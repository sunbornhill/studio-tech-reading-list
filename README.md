# Studio Technology Leadership Reading List

A living reading list for studio technology leadership, kept by Sunborn Hill Entertainment. It gets updated as new books, reports and talks are added.

Last updated 6 Nov 2026. Links were checked on 2 Nov 2026. Audible, PwC, Backstage, Bloomberg, Simon & Schuster, Unreal Engine and VES pages block automated checks, but each one appeared in live search results. In our own notes we say SI (Super Intelligence). Source titles keep their published wording.

## 1. Audible first: listen on the commute

Narrator and length come from Audible listings (or Apple Books, Google Play and OverDrive where noted) read on 2 Nov 2026.

1. [The Ride of a Lifetime, by Robert Iger](https://www.audible.com/pd/The-Ride-of-a-Lifetime-Audiobook/0525499334). Narrated by Jim Frangione and Robert Iger. 8 hrs 45 mins. Disney's CEO on taking on technology instead of fighting it, and on buying Pixar, Marvel and Lucasfilm. This is the starting point for a studio executive.
2. [Creativity, Inc. (The Expanded Edition), by Ed Catmull and Amy Wallace](https://www.audible.com/pd/Creativity-Inc-The-Expanded-Edition-Audiobook/B0BPDZTXWT). Narrated by Ed Catmull. 17 hrs 24 mins. Pixar's co-founder on running a company where technology and creative work meet. The 2023 edition adds new chapters.
3. [To Pixar and Beyond, by Lawrence Levy](https://www.audible.com/pd/To-Pixar-and-Beyond-Audiobook/B01M3VFVX0). Narrated by Bronson Pinchot. 7 hrs 54 mins. Pixar's CFO on turning a technology company into a studio, covering business models, the IPO and deal terms with Disney.
4. [No Rules Rules: Netflix and the Culture of Reinvention, by Reed Hastings and Erin Meyer](https://www.audible.com/pd/No-Rules-Rules-Audiobook/0753554720). Narrated by Jason Culp and Allyson Ryan. 9 hrs 42 mins. How a streamer's culture lets it move at technology speed.
5. [That Will Never Work, by Marc Randolph](https://www.audible.com/pd/That-Will-Never-Work-Audiobook/1549121669). Narrated by Marc Randolph. 11 hrs. Netflix's first CEO on the start-up years. A lesson in betting on a distribution technology before anyone else believes in it.
6. [Binge Times, by Dawn Chmielewski and Dade Hayes](https://www.audible.com/pd/Binge-Times-Audiobook/B09K4JZKLH). Narrated by Will Damron. 12 hrs 24 mins (OverDrive and audiobooks.com listings). How the legacy studios built streaming platforms to take on Netflix, and the technology and strategy costs.
7. [The Big Picture: The Fight for the Future of Movies, by Ben Fritz](https://www.audible.com/pd/The-Big-Picture-Audiobook/B07F1C3X3G). Narrated by Timothy Andrés Pabon. 9 hrs 44 mins. A WSJ reporter on the shift to franchises and streaming, drawing on the Sony emails. A clear look at the decisions studios face.
8. [DisneyWar, by James B. Stewart](https://www.audible.com/pd/DisneyWar-Audiobook/B076VTQ8L7). Narrated by Patrick Lawlor. 25 hrs 40 mins (from search results; not verified on the page itself). A warning about studio governance, boards and succession during the Eisner years.
9. [Powerhouse: The Untold Story of Hollywood's Creative Artists Agency, by James Andrew Miller](https://www.audible.com/author/James-Andrew-Miller/B00287PCCO). Narrated by James Andrew Miller, Kirby Heyborne and Ann Richardson. About 25 hrs (OverDrive and audiobooks.com listings). How talent power works from the agency side, which every studio executive negotiates against.

## 2. Articles and reports

**Pipeline and the MovieLabs 2030 Vision**
1. [MovieLabs: The 2030 Vision (hub)](https://movielabs.com/the-2030-vision/). The industry's shared plan for cloud-first, software-defined production, backed by the major studios.
2. [The Evolution of Media Creation (2030 Vision white paper, PDF)](https://www.movielabs.com/prodtech/ML_2030_Vision.pdf). The ten original principles that vendors and studios still measure themselves against.
3. [The Evolution of Production Workflows (Software-Defined Workflows, PDF)](https://movielabs.com/prodtech/sdw/ML_Software_Defined_Workflows.pdf). How automated, interoperable workflows replace manual handoffs, and where the savings come from.
4. [MovieLabs Ontology for Media Creation](https://movielabs.com/ontology-for-media-creation/). The shared data model for scripts, assets, tasks and people. It underpins pipeline and metadata decisions.
5. [Alliance for OpenUSD](https://aousd.org/). The standards body for USD, the scene-description format that VFX, animation and real-time pipelines are adopting.
6. [Variety: Motion Picture Academy Releases ACES 2.0 (2025)](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/academy-arts-sciences-aces-color-encoding-2-0-release-1236360406/). The current colour-management standard behind camera-to-delivery consistency.
7. [ASWF: ACES joins the Academy Software Foundation (2025)](https://www.aswf.io/news/aces/). Explains the open-source governance of the core tools studios depend on.

**Virtual production**
8. [Epic Games: Virtual Production Field Guide, Volume 2 (PDF)](https://cdn2.unrealengine.com/Virtual+Production+Field+Guide+Volume+2+v1.0-5b06b62cbc5f.pdf). Practical coverage of LED volumes and ICVFX, with interviews from working productions.
9. [Visual Effects Society: Virtual Production Publications](https://vesglobal.org/virtual-production-publications/). The VES's practitioner glossary and guides, useful for speaking the crew's language.
10. [SMPTE: On-Set Virtual Production (Rapid Industry Solutions)](https://www.smpte.org/rapid-industry-solutions/on-set-virtual-production). SMPTE's resources on standards, roles and interoperability for LED-stage work.

**SI and generative tools in production**
11. [Netflix Partner Help Center: Using Generative AI in Content Production](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/43393929218323-Using-Generative-AI-in-Content-Production). A major buyer's written rules for SI use: data, IP, talent consent and approvals. It's a ready template for our own policy.

**Distribution, rights and metadata**
12. [MovieLabs Digital Distribution Framework: Avails](https://www.movielabs.com/md/avails/). The standard format for windows and rights availability sent to platforms.
13. [EIDR (Entertainment Identifier Registry)](https://www.eidr.org/). The universal ID for titles and edits that keeps rights, delivery and reporting data aligned.
14. [Deloitte: Digital Media Trends](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/digital-media-trends-consumption-habits-survey.html). An annual survey of streaming churn, bundling and viewer behaviour, for distribution strategy.
15. [PwC: Global Entertainment & Media Outlook](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/industries/tmt/media/outlook.html). Five-year revenue forecasts by segment and country. This is the standard source for market-size figures.

**Tech budgeting and incentives**
16. [Olsberg SPI for the MPA: Global Film and Television Production Incentives (Nov 2024, PDF)](https://www.motionpictures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/GLOBAL_OlsbergSPI-Incentives-White-Paper_November-2024.pdf). How 120 tax-credit schemes work and how they shape where productions shoot.
17. [Olsberg SPI: Global Incentives Index, November 2025 update](https://www.o-spi.com/news/incentive-index-2025-november). The latest ranking of incentive schemes, to check before any location or VFX-vendor budget.
18. [Hollywood Professional Association (HPA)](https://www.hpaonline.com/). Home of the annual HPA Tech Retreat, where studio technology leaders present their roadmaps. The sessions are worth following.

## 3. Executives to study

1. **Rob Bredow**, Chief Creative Officer at ILM and SVP Creative Innovation at Lucasfilm (per [Lucasfilm leadership page](https://www.lucasfilm.com/leadership/rob-bredow/), as of Nov 2026). Study: [TED, "Star Wars changed visual effects — AI is doing it again" (2025)](https://www.ted.com/talks/rob_bredow_star_wars_changed_visual_effects_ai_is_doing_it_again). The StageCraft LED-volume story, and how a technology leader frames SI tools as serving artists.
2. **Elizabeth Stone**, Chief Product and Technology Officer at Netflix (as of July 2026). Study: [Lenny's Podcast interview](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/netflix-cpto-on-ai-and-the-future) and [Variety, as CTO (2024)](https://variety.com/2024/digital/news/netflix-cto-elizabeth-stone-ai-keeper-test-strictly-business-1236063776/). How a streamer's technology head organises teams, live events and SI work.
3. **Ted Sarandos**, co-CEO of Netflix (as of Oct 2025). Study: [Tools and Weapons with Brad Smith, "How tech transformed the way Netflix tells stories"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1ws5cjDXSGc) and [What's on Netflix on his SI comments](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/netflix-ai-strategy-ted-sarandos-interview/). A studio chief's view on balancing data with creative judgement.
4. **Rich Berger**, CEO of MovieLabs (as of the source). Study: [postPerspective, "Talking cloud with MovieLabs CEO Rich Berger"](https://postperspective.com/talking-cloud-with-movielabs-ceo-rich-berger/) and his [2030 Vision Fireside Chats podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2065529/episodes). The studios' joint technology agenda, explained by the person who runs it.
5. **Annie Chang**, VP Creative Technologies at Universal Pictures (as of the source). Study: [Backstage profile on Production 3.0](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/annie-chang-universal-vp-creative-tech-72165/). A studio-side programme to connect script, production, post and metadata.
6. **Paul Debevec**, Chief Research Officer at Eyeline Studios, powered by Netflix (as of Apr 2024). Study: [befores & afters, "The Paul Debevec long read"](https://beforesandafters.com/2024/04/04/the-paul-debevec-long-read/). The research behind accurate lighting on LED stages, from the leading figure in the field.
7. **Girish Balakrishnan**, Director of Virtual Production at Netflix (as of Aug 2020). Study: [Variety video, "Succeeding with Virtual Production for Entertainment and Brands"](https://variety.com/video/succeeding-with-virtual-production-for-entertainment-and-brands/). How a streamer scaled virtual production across its productions.
8. **Jeffrey Katzenberg**, co-founder of DreamWorks Animation (as of Nov 2023). Study: [TheWrap on his Bloomberg New Economy Forum remarks](https://www.thewrap.com/animated-films-cost-ai-jeffrey-katzenberg/) ([Bloomberg original](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-09/ai-will-cut-cost-of-animated-films-by-90-jeff-katzenberg-says)). A former studio chief's bold forecast on SI and animation costs. Read it critically, because it is a prediction, not data.
9. **Robert Iger**, CEO of The Walt Disney Company (per his book, 2019; check his current role). Study: *The Ride of a Lifetime* (above). The best first-hand account of a studio chief making technology bets.
10. **Ed Catmull**, co-founder and former president of Pixar. Study: *Creativity, Inc.* (above). A computer scientist who became a studio leader, and the strongest model for this role.

## 4. Leadership books (print or ebook)

1. [The Ride of a Lifetime, Robert Iger (Penguin Random House)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/545212/the-ride-of-a-lifetime-by-robert-iger/). Lessons on decisive studio leadership.
2. [Creativity, Inc. (Expanded Edition), Ed Catmull with Amy Wallace (Penguin Random House)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/216369/creativity-inc-the-expanded-edition-by-ed-catmull-with-amy-wallace/). How to build a culture that is both creative and technical.
3. [To Pixar and Beyond, Lawrence Levy (HarperCollins)](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/to-pixar-and-beyond-lawrence-levy). Studio finance and strategy from the CFO's chair.
4. [No Rules Rules, Reed Hastings and Erin Meyer (Penguin Random House)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/612149/no-rules-rules-by-reed-hastings-and-erin-meyer/). The culture behind a technology-first studio.
5. [That Will Never Work, Marc Randolph (Hachette)](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/marc-randolph/that-will-never-work/9780316530187/). An entrepreneur's view of disrupting distribution.
6. [The Big Picture, Ben Fritz (HarperCollins)](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-big-picture-ben-fritz). The modern economics of studios.
7. [DisneyWar, James B. Stewart (Simon & Schuster)](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/DisneyWar/James-B-Stewart/9781508251613). Governance and succession: what goes wrong at the top.
8. [Powerhouse, James Andrew Miller (HarperCollins)](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/powerhouse-james-andrew-miller). Talent and agency dynamics.
9. *Binge Times*, Dawn Chmielewski and Dade Hayes. See the Audible link above; the publisher page could not be confirmed.

## 5. Suggested study order (4 weeks)

- **Week 1: listen and orient.** Listen to *The Ride of a Lifetime* and *Creativity, Inc.* Read the 2030 Vision hub and white paper (items 1–2). Watch Bredow's TED talk.
- **Week 2: production technology.** Listen to *To Pixar and Beyond*. Read the Virtual Production Field Guide, VES, SMPTE, ACES 2.0, OpenUSD and Software-Defined Workflows (items 3–10). Read Debevec, Balakrishnan, Chang and Berger.
- **Week 3: streaming, distribution, data and SI.** Listen to *No Rules Rules*, *That Will Never Work* and *Binge Times*. Read Avails, EIDR, OMC, Deloitte and PwC (items 4, 12–15) and the Netflix generative-tools guidance (item 11). Watch Stone, Sarandos and Katzenberg.
- **Week 4: money, power and governance.** Listen to *The Big Picture*, then *DisneyWar* or *Powerhouse*. Read the Olsberg SPI incentives paper and the 2025 index (items 16–17). Browse the HPA Tech Retreat sessions (item 18). Finish by drafting a one-page SI and production-technology policy for Sunborn Hill, for internal use only.
